# Why we keep missing effects — the Effects agent's account (2026-09-30)

Written at Raven's request, from what is already known. No new investigation. Plain statements. Where I
have not verified something here, it says so.

I have not seen the EMC or Render accounts, so nothing below argues with them except where I quote a
message of theirs that I did see.

---

## 1. What each reported measure actually measured

| reported as | what it measured | what it did not measure |
|---|---|---|
| **"wired"** | the record is present in `docs/effects/<id>.json` | whether anything in the viewer ever fires it |
| **"0 refused"** | the records the recorder *tried* built without an `Unverified` throw | whether the right records were tried; whether any of them fire in play; whether the recording exercised the branches an animated pose reaches (the recorder poses a default, so animation-driven branches go unrecorded — this is a known, written limitation) |
| **"residue 0"** | every record in the monster's closed set had **a ROM firing site identified** | whether the viewer has a driver for it; whether the site is reachable in play |
| **"driven"** (introduced 2026-09-29) | something in the viewer fires it | whether it *draws*; whether it draws correctly; whether it fires at the right time |

**The three older words all measured the ROM side or the export side, and none measured the screen.** That
is the single largest cause. A monster could be signed with "residue 0, 0 refused, all wired" while a third
of its records had nothing to fire them, and that is exactly what happened: Rathian was signed on those
words, and 21 of its 74 records have no driver.

**The batch runs Raven called off (2026-09-28).** A script walked monsters and reported completion per
monster. What it measured was *that the script completed*. Basarios's six fireball records were absent and
the run reported success. Raven's reading — "It is supposed to be capturing monster specific effects and you
are not doing that" — was correct: the script's success criterion never mentioned the monster's own effects.
A second fault compounded it: **four copies of the batch ran concurrently**, which made monsters appear to
complete quickly, and I twice declared a live batch dead because a process filter matched `python.exe` when
the interpreter here is `python3.13.exe`.

**The 43% census.** I do not have that figure's derivation in front of me and will not reconstruct it from
memory. What I can say is that every census we produced counted **records exported or records with a ROM
site**, not records that appear, so any percentage from that period measures the same thing the word "wired"
measured and carries the same blind spot.

**Today's counts, tree-wide, from `dev/driven-split.mjs` at 4bc0750:**

    exported 5858   driven 4270   undriven 1588

    834  runtime exists, no control   blast band c 1130-1137, combat edges c 1200 / 1201
    426  event, no driver             the shared band, per-monster
    312  hyper                        deliberate: wired and must not fire
     13  shell record, no carrier     a `when: shell` record with no SHELL_DATA entry
      3  ef param 3 never selected    u 36 and two others
    ----
   1588  and the tally asserts its own sum against the tool's header

These numbers are three revisions deep (1583 → 1572 → 1681 → 1588). Each revision was a fault in my own
tool or my own reading of its output, not a change in the data. Section 3 lists them.

## 2. The triggering layers, and the gap in each

Counts are from the export and the tool; **approximate where marked**.

### PSL motion-bound (`when: 'clip'`) — 3167 records
The monster's `.psl` binds a record to a motion over a frame window. Viewer: `CLIP_EFFECTS` in
`render/monster.js`, generated by `efx/add_effects.py`. **Driven wherever the clip exists** — and a test of
6957 bindings found **zero** whose clip is missing or whose frames fall outside what ships. This layer is in
good shape. Its known limitation is the export rule: a record whose PSL frame count differs from the clip's
is left out (Barroth's step-splash `em044_00c 130` is absent over a three-frame mismatch), and those
omissions are silent.

### Shared enemy code (`when: 'event'`) — 2361 records, **426 undriven**
Ids resolve through two shared base tables (`0x159c798` c, `0x159c7fc` u, one block, u starting 25 words in,
84 keys). The ROM fires these from shared code on state changes, procs and reactions. Viewer: nothing
generic — each one needs a hand-written row in `MOTION_STATES`, or a control, or nothing fires it. **This is
the largest real gap.** Concentrations: `c 1500` 90 monsters, `c 1105` 49, `c 1106` 33, `c 1109` 31,
`c 1103` 31, `c 1101` 30, `c 1102` 30, `c 1104` 27, `u 1400` 26, `u 1000` 18, `u 905` 17, `u 900` 14,
`u 1001` 13, then a tail of 11 keys.

### Class request sites — 690 id→key rows across 44 classes, 51 tables
A class overrides vtable `+0x1cc` (c.pel) / `+0x1d0` (u.pel); an id ≥ 1000 resolves as `table[id-1000]`, a
WORD array in `.rodata`. 13 of 63 monster classes make no request at all. Viewer: **no general mechanism.**
A requested record reaches the screen only if some hand-written row or control happens to name it. Basarios's
six ids 1001-1006 are the worked example: read end to end, and still not on screen.

### Shells — 131 records on 11 monsters
`SHELL_DATA` in `render/shells.js` (EMC's), driven by `stepShells`. Each shell mode carries an `ef` list
whose index is the trigger: **0 = flight, 1/2/3 = landing by hit type, 4/5 = bounces**. Two structural gaps:
`landing()` selects `[1,2,3][hit.type]` and `hitType()` returns only 0 or 1, so **ef param 3 is unreachable
in the viewer** (hit type 2 is a hit on a hunter, and there are none) — that is why `em001_00 u 36` never
fires. And a monster can have shell-carried records with **no `SHELL_DATA` entry at all** (13 records;
Basarios's four were in this state until 2026-09-29), in which case nothing spawns.

### Second-generation shells — not enumerable
`ctx.create` (`shells.js`, "the shells its shells make", 0x48b884) spawns further shells whose **mode is
computed at runtime**. A record can therefore fire on a mode no action row names. Render watched one
`shell00` mode-8 action on `em001_00 L4 Motion[16]` produce `u 31 / u 32 / u 33 / u 34`, which live on
`shell01` modes 6/7/8/9. **No static analysis of the tables can decide this layer**; only observation can.

### State-driven (`MOTION_STATES`, `RAGE_PUFF`, `CUT_TAIL`, the shared-state panel)
`ragePuff` 183 records, `rage` 13, `rageStart` 2, `calm` 1 — these are driven by their own layer. The panel
in `index.html` offers exactly **two** toggles (`c1100`, `c1108`). The runtime has working methods for more
and nothing calls them: `blastPart(part)` has **no caller anywhere in the tree**, and `combat(from, to)`
only its own wrapper. That is the whole of the 834 "runtime exists, no control" figure — the blast band and
the two combat edges, on ~90 monsters each. **Those are the cheapest records in the tree to recover: the
code works, nobody wired a control.**

### Deliberate non-firing
Hyper band (312) is wired and must not fire, by decision. `c 1106` (33) and `c 1500` (90) were removed by
Raven on what the game does. These are correct and should not be counted as gaps — but they were inside
"undriven" until today, which made the number look worse than it is.

## 3. Findings I withdrew, grouped by cause

### A fit reported as a read
* **MASK1 = payload +0x48.** Found by searching every 4-byte offset pair for one that yielded plausible row
  sets, and reported as established. It later survived a proper read (the ROM's own copy `0x31c4ac`, block
  offsets = payload + 0x10, corroborated at four anchors), but it was a fit when I reported it. PM was right
  to refuse it.
* **`f104` as the emission count.** A real field name with a plausible set of values and **no consumer
  anywhere in the lifted runtime**. Withdrawn.
* **payload +0x60 as a colour.** Held back rather than claimed. Still unread.

### A tool checked by an untested one-liner
* **`grep -c` counting lines as records** — reported "59 clip-missing records" when the same run contained
  93. The number never moved; my measurement did.
* **Splitting output on `", "` when the reason strings contain commas** — dropped 834 records and reported
  categories summing to 1255 of 1681. PM's arithmetic check caught it.

### A truncated print read as absence
* **All 93 "clip missing" records.** The test accepted `Motion[N]` and `Motion[N]_start` but not
  `Motion[N]_loop`. I "verified end to end" on `em002_00 L4 Motion[29]` by printing clips beginning
  `Motion[2` **sliced to 8 entries**, with `Motion[29]_loop` past the cut. 93 false, 0 genuine. The check
  existed to catch that bug and was written so that it could only agree with me.

### A guard shallower than the claim it guarded
* **"shells.js has exactly one `spawn()` call site and it takes an action"** — offered as proof I had not
  over-corrected. It is the *first generation* only; `ctx.create` is the second. I grepped one pattern and
  treated it as an enumeration.

### A rule applied across monsters
* **Shell mode reachability.** Seven false negatives on Rathian alone. Removed entirely, not patched: the
  layer is not statically decidable.
* **Hardcoded shared-band ranges stopping at 1009** (residue 231 → 306), and **the union of all class
  tables instead of the serving class** (306 → 280). Both from before today.

### A record's identity collapsed
* **`fired_map` keyed on `(list, array, key)`** — collapsed six Glavenus rows and lost their modes. Found by
  EMC. `(pel, key)` is not unique either: 45 duplicate groups tree-wide.
* **`space1.py` merging records by key** — fixing it with `(pel, key, index)` *reproduced* the bug; only
  adding `path` gave the true answer.

### A detector matching the setup and not the use
* **Request sites matched on `ldr [rY,#0x1d0]` without the `blx`** — gave Rathian a phantom site at
  `0xd05e80`, a field copy, and inflated an all-class scan.
* **Class code ranges overrunning by 0x4000** — every class inherited its neighbour's identity.
* **`cmp` immediates collected without register tracking** — gave Khezu eight em numbers.

### A verification that could not fail
* **The placement proof.** "Anchors exactly equal joint positions under a 90° yaw and 1.4 scale" used three
  records whose offsets are all **(0, 0, 0)** — invariant under any rotation. It proved the anchor lands on
  its joint and could not have tested the transform. The `host.js +0xf0` fix it was cited for is
  **correct-and-untested**.

### My own notes trusted, or incomplete
* **The row gate.** I described it as `(w00 & MASK1) && (w04 & MASK2)` for months. It has a **third,
  prior exclusion test** on `(effect+0x116 | global+0x228) & row[+4]`. My Basarios row sets came out right
  anyway, which was luck.
* **`+0x110..+0x118` described as a packed property group** with `+0x116` as a half-word — the loader shows
  three consecutive per-entry bytes. Withdrawn.
* **payload +0x38 "space"** sat in my notes with meanings I refused to lean on until read. That refusal was
  correct and the read later confirmed it. This is the one case where distrusting my own notes paid.

### A comment trusted over a read, or a judgement inherited
* I nearly left `c 1109` out because a comment said "the break is the only one of the three with anything to
  see". It is a real record and two other monsters fire it on that clip.
* `c 1105`'s period 42 — I first justified it as "all 27 monsters use it". That is an observation about rows
  other agents authored, not a reading. **I could not find 42 in the ROM** and the comment now says so.

### Bugs in my own instrument that looked like findings
* `D.lists` undefined on entries relying on the default `{0: u, 1: c}` — condemned four of Nargacuga's spike
  records as "named by no shell mode".
* "No `CLIP_EFFECTS` bit found" treated as proof nothing fires it — 6 records.

**The shape repeats: I measured the output with a shortcut whose assumptions I never tested, and the tool was
right each time.** That is four separate instances in one day.

## 4. The hand-authored tables

`MOTION_STATES` is written per monster by hand, from the command layer's point of view. Three failure modes,
all observed:

**(a) Written from the model's point of view, not the effect's.** Gravios's head row is
`levels: [[3], [3], [4]], fire: [null, null, u 1026]` and its comment says "Level 1 shows nothing at G".
That is true *of the model* — levels 0 and 1 share set `[3]` — but the ROM still fires a **level-1 record**:
`index = part*5 + level + 6` puts part 5 level 1 at `u 1025`. Same on `G_BACK` for part 0 level 1 (`u 1000`).
Two records, loaded and never fired, on a monster whose block is otherwise carefully authored and even cites
the right script addresses.

**(b) Only part of a known answer transcribed.** Diablos's comment states "(10, 0x6e) plays L3 Motion[9] and
then holds L3 Motion[13], c 1105 every 42". Only the **hold** ever got a row. The play clip — the one the
record fires on — had none, so `c 1105` was loaded and never fired. The answer was in the file for months.

**(c) A judgement recorded as a fact, then inherited.** The exhaust was left out of Diablos's row on the
reasoning "the break is the only one of the three with anything to see". Later readers (me included) treat a
comment that records a decision as a finding, and do not re-derive it.

**How many more are like this.** `u 1000` is undriven on **18** monsters and `u 1001` on **13**. Both sit in
exactly position (b)/(a) — a break record at a level whose set does not change. I expect **a substantial
fraction of those 31 to be the same omission**, not genuine absence. Add `c 1101`–`c 1105` and `c 1109`
(~200 records) on monsters with no states table at all. Estimate, marked as such: **of the 426
"event, no driver" records, I would guess 150–250 are recoverable by completing existing rows or writing a
first row, and the remainder are deliberate or need a decode.** That is a guess from the pattern, not a
count.

## 5. How the work was organised

**Lanes cut across the question.** A record's *data* is mine; the *clip it fires on* is the command layer's;
whether it *appears* is Render's. No single lane could answer "does this monster's effects work", and the
sign-off words each lane used measured its own half. Five break clips sat blocked on EMC for a day while a
**validated script reader already existed in the repo** (`efx/agents/cephadrome-scratch/scr.py`) that
answered them in minutes. Nobody had a map of the tooling; I found it by guessing a directory name.

**Sign-off criteria measured the wrong thing, and were accepted for months.** "wired / residue 0 / 0 refused"
was sufficient to close a monster. The first time anyone asked "how many of these are ever fired" was
2026-09-29, and the answer was 22 of Rathian's 34 event records were not. Every monster closed before that
needs re-checking, and the board now says so.

**Observation was introduced last.** Render's `requestEffect` hook is the only instrument that has caught a
real gap by seeing it. It arrived after ~40 monsters were signed. Worse, its first version hooked
`schedule.start`, which shell effects never pass through, and reported 8 shell records as never firing —
**I then built a static test on that withdrawn finding and produced seven false negatives.** A retracted
observation propagated into a tool.

**Siblings hide errors.** Variants share their base's data and LMT. `em019_00`/`em019_04`, `em020_00`/`_04`
show *identical* dead sites because they index the same table. An error in a base silently doubles.

**The PM's part, directly.** PM produced most of today's real corrections: the exported-vs-driven split, the
arithmetic check that caught my 1255, refusing `+0x48` as a fit, demanding a ROM address for the period,
and rejecting my re-report against Render's screen. That is the most effective supervision this work has had.
Three things worked against it:

* **"One line to me after each step" and "do not stop between the steps".** Both of my worst reports —
  the 93 and the mode-reachability re-report — went out to meet a per-step reporting cadence, before I had
  tested the measurement. The pause where verification happens is exactly what those instructions remove.
* **Praise before checking.** The 93 was recorded as "a separate and good finding" and scheduled for
  follow-up work before anyone tested it. It was worth nothing. An instruction to verify it would have cost
  five minutes.
* **Re-ordering.** The queue changed direction roughly ten times in a day (Basarios → gate writers → tool →
  Diablos → gate → tool → break clips). Several threads are now parked three reads deep. Fragmenting a
  decode is expensive because the cost is in re-establishing the context, not in the read.

**And the part that is mine, without the framing.** I reported unverified numbers. The cadence is context,
not an excuse. Four measurement errors in one day is my count, not anyone else's.

## 6. What I would change, in priority order

**1. Get a ground truth from the running game. I believe this is feasible and it is the only thing that
fixes the class of error rather than instances of it.**

What it would be: for one monster, a list of `(action or state) -> effect record` pairs produced by running
the ROM, not by reading tables.

Why it is feasible — the pieces all exist and are already used:
* `efx/agents/notice-scratch/probe.py` already runs a class's action main under unicorn with
  `enemy+0x73e0/+0x73e1` set and reports where it stops. It resolves actions for 98 classes.
* `efx/agents/cephadrome-scratch/scr.py` already walks the action scripts to their motions.
* `efx/proof.py` and the recorder already run the ROM's *effect* path under the emulator and check the
  lifted runtime against recorded vectors.
* The request funnel is narrow: vtable `+0x1cc` / `+0x1d0` and the two shared bases `0xa3dc0` / `0xa3ddc`.
  **Logging every call through those four while sweeping actions × parts × states would produce the list
  directly.**

What it would take: a harness that sweeps `(status, number, part, direction, posture)` per class and logs
request calls rather than stopping at the first motion. The real cost is the enemy struct — today's work
alone turned up `+0x116`, `+0x1054`, `+0x1428`, `+0x5ccb`, `+0x73e0/1`, `+0xb5f4/5`, `+0x2c0`, `+0x518`,
`+0x505` as fields a branch reads, and a sweep needs plausible values for each or it takes the wrong arm
silently. That is days, not hours, and it needs a validation set — the monsters Render has signed by
observation.

Its honest limit: it gives **reachable from some action**, not **reachable in play**. Which action the AI
picks is unread, and nothing in this plan changes that.

**2. Make observation the sign-off and the tool a prediction.** Render's hook is ground truth. A monster
closes on "N records seen firing, M named as not, with reasons" — never on an export count. Where my tool
and the screen disagree, the screen is right and my tool is the thing to fix. This is already PM's rule as
of today; it should be the permanent one.

**3. Recover the 834 cheapest records first.** The blast band and the combat edges have working runtime code
and no caller. Two controls would move ~834 records on ~90 monsters. Nothing else in this document has that
ratio. It needs Raven's decision on whether those states should be user-toggleable, and that decision has
been pending since 2026-09-29.

**4. Complete the authored rows systematically rather than per monster.** For every break row with a null
`fire` slot, check whether the shared-table record for that `(part, level)` exists. That is mechanical, it
is the same shape as the two I fixed on Gravios, and it plausibly covers 31+ records.

**5. Never report a number a script has not parsed with an assertion in it.** Concretely: the tally script
asserts its categories sum to the tool's own header, and it is tested against a run whose answer is already
known before being pointed at a new one. That rule, applied a day earlier, prevents three of today's four
errors.

**6. Index the tooling.** A one-page map of what exists in `efx/` and `efx/agents/*` and what each answers.
`scr.py` cost a day.

### What I would stop doing

* **Stop using the word "wired".** It has meant "exported" while being read as "works". Say exported,
  driven, or seen.
* **Stop reporting per step.** Report per verified finding. A slower cadence with a check in it is cheaper
  than a fast one that has to be withdrawn — today's withdrawals cost more time than the checks would have.
* **Stop naming a field from the shape of its values.** EMC's rule, learned the same day from the other
  side: a field's meaning comes from the instruction that reads it, or it stays unread.
* **Stop building on a finding without re-deriving it.** A retracted observation became my static test.
  A comment recording a judgement became a fact. Both are the same error.
* **Stop treating a negative from a naming convention as a negative about the data.** `_loop` versus
  `_start`, `<stem>_*.dds` versus the `.mrl`'s own texture names: twice in one day.
